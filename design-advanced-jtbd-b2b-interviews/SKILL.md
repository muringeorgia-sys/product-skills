# Design Advanced JTBD B2B Interviews

Work in the user's language. Treat every job statement as a hypothesis until research evidence confirms it.

## Goal

Turn a short B2B research request into a decision-ready interview design, not a generic list of questions. Identify the research route first, ask only the missing routing questions, then produce a tailored moderator guide.

Use [routing and question bank](references/routing-and-question-bank.md) for every design. Also use [solution interview and Think Aloud](references/solution-interview-and-think-aloud.md) whenever the design includes a concept, prototype, sales material, price, or commitment test.

## First response: diagnose before designing

Extract, if present: research decision, product or alternative to study, candidate respondent, candidate job, desired outcome, moment of use or purchase, and available test materials.

1. Formulate one to three candidate job hypotheses. Prefer `When [situation], [role] wants to [make progress], so that [outcome]`. Do not phrase a job as a feature, a solution, or an abstract company goal.
2. Independently classify the target as **recurring/frequency** or **sequential** and state the reasoning in one sentence. A recurring job is repeatedly performed in comparable cycles; a sequential job is a step whose order relative to preceding and following work is material.
3. Always ask the user to confirm or correct that classification, even when it seems obvious. Do not infer recurrence solely from repeated use of a solution: a repeated process can still contain a target sequential step.
4. State a preliminary interview type and ask for confirmation if the objective is not explicit:
   - **Problem interview**: reconstruct actual past behavior, job, context, alternatives, unmet outcomes, and buying process.
   - **Solution interview**: observe reaction to a concrete offer and test willingness to take a costly B2B next step.
   Mentioning a product or competitor alone does not make an interview a solution interview.
5. Ask only the missing questions from the intake below, in one batch of at most five. Do not ask for information already in the brief. Use a choice UI with a recommended option when available; otherwise number the answers so the user can reply compactly.

### Minimum intake

Ask these in priority order when unknown:

1. **Job structure — always ask:** “Предварительно это [частотная / последовательная] работа, потому что […]. Подтвердите: изучаем повторяющийся цикл или конкретный шаг в цепочке работ?”
2. **Starting point:** “Что известно на старте: (а) гипотеза работы, но не решение; (б) решение, но не работа; (в) и работа, и решение; (г) пока ни то ни другое?”
3. **Research intent:** “Нужно понять реальный текущий опыт и проблему или проверить конкретное решение/питч/цену и готовность двигаться к покупке?”
4. **Respondent and unit:** role, company or segment, and whether the person performs the work, selects a solution, influences, approves, pays, implements, or supports it.
5. **Evidence boundary:** most recent relevant event, required decision after the research, and available materials for a solution test (prototype, pitch, landing page, price, or none).

If neither a job nor a solution is known, ask the user to narrow the scope to a role plus a recent behavior, decision, or business situation before producing a full guide. If the work has both recurring and sequential aspects, choose one as the interview anchor and name the other as a secondary lens.

Do not output a final questionnaire while a mandatory routing answer is missing. Output the provisional hypothesis and the compact intake instead.

## Build the design after intake

Return the following sections, using the exact context rather than placeholders:

1. **Research frame:** decision to make, target segment, research question, assumptions, and out-of-scope items.
2. **Routing decision:** problem, solution, or a deliberately split hybrid; recurring or sequential; known/unknown job and solution. Explain the route briefly.
3. **Job graph scope:** higher-level outcome, target job, and the prior/next, prior-version, or lower-level branch to explore. Do not present the graph as a validated fact.
4. **Recruitment and screener:** inclusion/exclusion criteria, recency requirement, role in the B2B buying/using system, and disqualifiers. Keep job performer and economic buyer separate unless they are demonstrably the same person.
5. **Session plan:** recommended duration, phases, timeboxes, materials, and roles present. Make timeboxes approximate.
6. **Moderator guide:** for every phase, provide the primary neutral question, concise probes, the evidence to capture, and a branch condition. Select questions from the bank; do not dump the entire bank.
7. **B2B evidence map:** user, champion, influencer, selector, approver, payer, implementer, and support owner as relevant; also capture procurement, security, integrations, and adoption constraints only when relevant.
8. **Notes and synthesis:** a compact coding frame and a decision rule. Separate observed facts, respondent interpretations, and researcher inferences.
9. **Risks:** leading wording, recall bias, wrong respondent, mixed jobs, exposure to a concept too early, and lack of authority for the requested commitment.

For a hybrid, keep the problem evidence and the solution reactions in separate blocks. Do not let exposure to the proposed solution rewrite the reconstruction of past behavior.

## Moderation rules

- Anchor problem interviews in a specific, recent episode: “Вспомните последний раз… Что произошло сначала?” Then probe sequence, artifacts, people, timing, alternatives, and consequences.
- Ask about past behavior and real trade-offs before opinions or hypothetical future behavior. Replace “Would you use/pay?” with questions about what was actually done, bought, approved, or rejected.
- Do not introduce the candidate solution or job wording until the respondent has described the relevant experience, unless the route intentionally starts from known solution use.
- In B2B, distinguish the person’s evidence from their authority. Never ask a non-buyer to promise a purchase; request only a next step they can genuinely take.
- A refusal in a solution interview is evidence. Ask what prevented the next step and what was missing; do not defend the product.
- Mark every unverified classification, job, and causal claim as an assumption in the final design.

## Quality check before delivery

Verify that the guide has one clear anchor, uses neutral past-oriented wording, includes a branch for the selected work structure, and identifies the relevant B2B roles. For a solution interview, verify that a concrete test asset, Think Aloud setup, comparison basis, and a realistic costly action are all present. State what still requires the user's answer.
