# Mentor rules for case answers

Read only for answer review, mock interview, or a request for interview-specific feedback.

1. Start from the business goal, not from a feature or metric list.
2. Ask two or three clarifying questions, then proceed; do not turn the interview into an intake form.
3. Think aloud and name assumptions. A case tests process, not a single correct answer.
4. Segment before choosing a problem; include people who never used the product when growth is the goal.
5. Choose the segment using an explicit business criterion: size, value, or influence on the metric.
6. Use qualitative research to find problem hypotheses, then quantitative evidence to size or compare them when appropriate.
7. Do not over-explain respondent recruiting unless it changes the decision; focus on why this evidence is needed.
8. Prioritize one problem and one solution with stated criteria. Equal priority is usually a missing criterion, not a reason to build everything.
9. Before a large build, test the minimum version on a prototype, pilot, usability test, A/B test, or valid alternative.
10. Name a metric that matches the affected segment and funnel step. Keep limiting metrics proportional to the change.
11. For metric questions, operationalize the answer: goal -> metric tree -> unit of observation -> aggregation and segments -> decision.
12. Do not state an invented effect size or use a term whose meaning you cannot explain.
13. In a broad stakeholder or legacy-product situation, do not promise to collect and deliver every request. State the product vision, set time-bound target outcomes, and narrow competing demands to one or two focus areas using explicit criteria.

These are feedback heuristics from mentor material and interview audits, not universal laws. Apply them to the context.
