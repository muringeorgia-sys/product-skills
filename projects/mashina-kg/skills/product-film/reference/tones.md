# Tones and the hook

The product's design and voice still decide colors, type and words. A tone sets energy, pacing and transitions on top of them. Pick the one nearest to the user's direction and keep their own words ("fake Series A launch from 2016") in the film prompt; a freeform direction refines the preset.

| Tone | Feel | Scenes and pacing | Transitions | Hook shape |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `default` | Punchy, playful, clean | 4–5 scenes, 3–5 s each | clean slide or cut | an observation that sets up the reveal |
| `polished` | Serious, elegant, restrained | 3–4 scenes, long holds | slow fade 0.6–0.8 s or camera move | the product's promise or name at full scale |
| `yc-parody` | Deadpan launch, played straight | 4–5 scenes, one claim each | hard cuts | the problem stated completely seriously |
| `chaotic` | Fast, loud, all caps | 6–8 scenes, some under 2 s | flash and zoom cuts | a shout that lands on the first beat |
| `deadpan` | Calm, dry, nothing is a joke | 3–4 scenes, big empty space | slow fades | a flat statement of an absurd fact |
| `cinematic` | Trailer scale, big claims | 4–5 scenes, big type | dramatic wipes | a slow build to one huge line |
| `app-store` | Clean feature cards | 4–6 scenes | smooth slides | the product already working, one feature per card |

The quality floor and the product's own language override a tone's pacing, casing and transitions: `chaotic` still holds every line long enough to read, and no tone adds devices or effects the product does not use.

With no direction: `polished` for serious products and anything sold to clients, `default` for playful consumer apps.

## The hook (first 2 seconds)

Plan it before anything else: it decides whether anyone keeps watching. Strong hooks from real runs:
- the product's own headline set large, word by word, with its accent color arriving under the key phrase, then the camera pulling back to reveal it is the live page;
- the pain in one line of the buyer's words, before the product appears;
- the result already on screen (the answer, the finished report), then rewind to how it happened.

Something moves in the first second. The first words are readable by 1.5 s.

## Humor

It comes from the product's own absurdity, not from trying. A serious product gets no jokes; a ridiculous one gets played straight.
